# Video Prompt Style Guide

Use this file before writing or reviewing any video prompt for the Baby English Island level videos.

## Non-negotiables

- The video is for 3-5 year old zero-beginner English learners.
- The target English word must be visually unambiguous before and while it is spoken.
- When the target word is spoken, the matching person, object, body part, animal, or action must be the only clear foreground meaning target.
- Do not place another learnable object near the child, in the child's hands, or in the foreground during or immediately before the target word.
- No subtitles, captions, written labels, flashcards, classroom UI, teacher-style drills, or narrator-heavy explanation.
- Keep speech Pre-A1 / very young A1: 1-5 words per turn, slow, clear, natural English.

## Emotional Direction

- Each prompt should feel like a tiny family memory, not a vocabulary demo.
- Use a small emotional need that a mother recognizes: being missed, being helped, being comforted, sharing food, waiting patiently, feeling brave, coming home, being noticed, or being gently protected.
- End with a warm payoff: eye contact, a soft smile, a hug, hand-holding, a shared laugh, a proud nod, or a quiet moment of closeness.
- Keep emotion specific and restrained. No melodrama, crying performance, sales language, or moralizing.
- The strongest purchase trigger is: "My child could learn English inside a tender, safe, beautiful world."

## Visual Style

- Hand-painted watercolor and gouache storybook animation matching the current Magic English Voyage island art direction.
- Soft pencil outlines, visible paper grain, warm cream sunlight, pale turquoise sea, moss green plants, coral-pink accents, low-saturation colors.
- Characters should look warm, clean, attractive, and child-safe: bright eyes, soft cheeks, balanced proportions, gentle smiles.
- Avoid glossy 3D, plastic CGI, hard vector outlines, neon colors, parody anime, logos, signatures, and readable text.

## Prompt Pattern

1. Title
2. Duration and format
3. Cinematic style
4. Source situation
5. CEFR target
6. Zero-beginner clarity rule
7. Scene
8. Characters
9. Story container
10. Dialogue or dialogue beats
11. Action and performance
12. Camera
13. Audio
14. Negative prompt

## Preflight Check

Before generating video, confirm:

- The target word is tied to exactly one visual meaning.
- The child is not holding a confusing object when saying the target word.
- Foreground props cannot be mistaken for the target word.
- The scene has one warm emotional beat that would matter to a mother.
- The dialogue sounds like family life, not a lesson script.
- The final frame leaves a tender human feeling.
